# Stick With Eddie — channel bible

Short reference for everything decided so far. Update as things change.

## NEW DIRECTION (replaces the old format)
- **Shorts only.** No more long-form episodes. Every upload is one vertical Short (9:16, ~45–65 s).
- **Fun facts and true stories, not "What If".** Random, surprising facts told as a little story — history, inventions, science, odd jobs, clever tricks, strange laws. They don't have to be the same facts as the reference channel.
- **Inspiration:** Ricks Clips (@RicksClips3000). We borrow the format and craft, not the scripts or designs.
- **Every topic gets its own world.** Build whatever the story needs: the places, buildings, objects, props and characters (with costumes for the era or job).
- **Smooth transitions** from place to place, with different animations and characters in each scene.
- The older "What If" episodes below stay as history and as a library of sets, characters and animation to reuse.

## What Ricks Clips does (from his top 10 Shorts)
Based on still frames from the 10 most-viewed Shorts (10–34 million views each) and two full transcripts. I could not download the videos, so cut timing and camera moves are estimated from stills.

**Topics (examples of the kind of thing that works)**
- The ancient jar that warned an empire (an earthquake detector) · Why perfect railroads don't work (gaps for heat) · The job nobody wants in Japan · Your brain's secret idea zone · Why sailors carried storm oil · The window that breaks Japan's gambling law · What the grave bell really meant · The shape that saved roller coasters · The pink prison cell experiment · Levels of pirate cannonballs.
- Pattern: one odd object, rule or decision with a hidden reason. Title is a short curiosity gap, 4–7 words, no clickbait punctuation.

**Script shape (~65 s, about 190 words)**
1. **0–4 s hook:** a concrete action by "he", no setup. "He put a bronze ball in a dragon's mouth and told the emperor: if this ever falls out, run."
2. **"You see…"** then the situation in two or three lines.
3. **Conflict:** someone powerful laughs at or punishes the hero (the emperor, the boss, the guards).
4. **Escalation in threes:** it happens once, nothing; again, they laugh harder; then it happens for real.
5. **A line of dialogue or two** in a character's voice for humour.
6. **Payoff in the very last sentence**, which also explains how the thing works. Ends cold — no "subscribe", no outro.
- Plain words, present or simple past tense, short sentences, a new beat every 2–4 seconds.

**Look**
- **Characters:** flat cartoon cut-outs with thick black outlines — round white head, pill-shaped coloured body, thin stick arms and legs, simple dot eyes with expressive brows. Costumes carry the story: uniforms, hats, moustaches, armour, period clothes. Crowds of extras react (laughing, pointing, glaring).
- **Worlds:** full 3D sets with real perspective — textured floors and walls, signage, furniture, vehicles, landscapes. Strong coloured lighting per scene (warm lamps, blue night, sunset, pink room), cast shadows, glow, fog and depth.
- **Camera:** very dynamic. Low angles, over-the-shoulder, extreme close-ups on faces, wide establishing shots, a foreground object close to the lens for depth, quick push-ins. A new angle or place every 2–4 s.
- **Objects as heroes:** the key object gets its own close-up or cutaway (inside the jar, the rail gap, the phone screen).
- **Captions:** small, one or two words at a time, white with a black outline, centred in the lower-middle. No big coloured bars.
- **Occasional real photo or footage** insert for a real-world example.
- **Audio:** one calm, clear narrator; light background music; sound effects on actions.

## Our version of that format
- **Host:** Eddie stays as the channel's face — he plays the hero of each story in costume (engineer, sailor, guard, inventor). Extras are the same stick family with outfits and accessories.
- **Length:** 45–65 s. **Structure:** hook → "You see…" → conflict → rule of three → payoff. No call to action inside the video.
- **3D sets for every story,** built per topic and kept in a growing library so later Shorts reuse them (already built: library, sunset rooftop, cozy room).
- **Transitions between places:** camera moves that carry you (push through a door or window, whip pan, match cut on a shape, zoom into an object and out into the next scene), not plain cuts.
- **Variety per scene:** a different action, angle and character group every scene; props and buildings that match the topic.
- **Captions:** switch to the small one-or-two-word style.
- **Facts must be true:** every Short lists its source in the description.

## Script and hooks (still useful for first lines)
- **Open mid-action or in the past, never at "day one".** Ep 2 opens on the Day 30 rooftop ("…somebody just thought back"), Day 1 starts by ~0:13.
- **Hook patterns** (full list in `content/HOOKS.md`): "Is it possible to [goal] in [time]?", "It's currently [time] and I just promised…", "I don't know who needs to hear this, but…", "[x] days ago I…", "If you've been [painful moment]…", and the Ricks Clips formula: concrete action in the first 4 s, then "You see…".
- **Story arc:** start high, then a new dip-and-spike every 8–15 s (question, twist or quiz); no flat stretch over ~12 s; payoff in the last line.
- **Every episode has:** day stamps, a Day 1→30 progress bar, two quizzes with a 3-2-1 countdown, fact cards with real sources, a twist, and a teaser for the next episode.
- **Shorts:** own first line, 13–35 s, end card "FULL STORY ON THE CHANNEL", set the full episode as the related video.

## Editing style (2D)
- 30 fps, every frame animated. Easing, anticipation and overshoot on pose changes (see `MOTION.md`).
- **Walking:** feet plant and roll, stride matches distance, both shoes face the walking direction.
- **Acting:** characters always do something real (hand over a coffee, high-five, fist bump, deal cards, read, sip, scroll a phone). No arm-waving. Idle characters stand still.
- **Mouths** follow the narration; blinks; small reaction icons (!, ?, sweat, sparkle).
- **Thought/speech bubbles:** pop in on the exact word, tail always points at the thinker, never cover a face, stay fully in frame.
- **Captions:** 1–3 bold words, white with black outline, spoken word turns yellow, numbers green. Placed where they cover no character (usually the floor strip).
- **Backgrounds:** every set is richly detailed (shelves, plants, lamps, posters, rugs, ambient motion). Explainer/quiz shots use a cozy tinted room.
- **Camera:** wide/medium/close cuts every ~2 s, steady on walking shots, crafted push-ins on key moments.
- **Motion graphics:** speed lines when running, sound-wave rings when covering ears, bursts on contact, confetti, kinetic captions.

## How it's made
- Code lives in `assets/characters/eddie/`. An episode is one "beats" file (`content/epNN-*.beats.mjs`): each beat has a pose, scene, people, bubbles, camera and the narration line.
- `render-episode.mjs <beats> <outDir> [--aspect 9:16] [--3d]` makes the voice, timing, captions, sound and video.
- Key files: `eddie-rig.mjs` (character), `scenes*.mjs` + `decor.mjs` (2D sets), `sets3d.js` + `stage3d.js` (3D), `build-brand.mjs` (thumbnails), `content/ep02-package.md` (title/description/chapters).
- Finished videos go in `deliver/`; working files in `projects/` (neither is committed).
